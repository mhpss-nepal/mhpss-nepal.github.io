Mental Health & Psychosocial Support · Rasuwa–Bhote Koshi flood response

The purpose of the Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) Sub-Cluster is to enable coordination and information sharing between MHPSS actors responding to the Rasuwa–Bhote Koshi floods, to work towards one shared picture of who is doing what and where, to make referral pathways usable across organisations, and to apply the IASC Guidelines on MHPSS in Emergency Settings to this response.

The flood response

The question this coordination exists to answer

Not how many people were reached in total — that number can look healthy while a whole holding centre has nobody. The question is which sites on the official roster have received no mental health or psychosocial service at all, and that can only be answered if every organisation reports against the same roster.

About

Who the sub-cluster is open to

The MHPSS Sub-Cluster is open to government bodies, UN agencies, international and national NGOs, professional associations, academic departments, and interested organisations or individuals active in mental health and psychosocial support in Nepal.

Mental health and psychosocial support in Nepal has long been coordinated through two tracks — a mental health sub-cluster under the Health Cluster, and a psychosocial working group under Protection. Organisations whose primary mandate is not MHPSS but whose work carries mental health or psychosocial components — protection, child protection, gender-based violence, education, nutrition, camp management — are encouraged to attend and contribute. One shared table does not ask anyone to leave their own.

Government

  • Ministry of Health and Food Safety (MoHP)

Membership is listed by category rather than by name until the sub-cluster confirms its own participant list. Naming an organisation here before it has agreed to be named would be a claim, not a record.

Activities

What the group does

🤝

Coordination

A standing meeting and one shared table for who is doing what and where, so the health and protection tracks can see the same picture without either giving up its own reporting line.

📚

Technical resources

The IASC Guidelines on MHPSS in Emergency Settings, the MHPSS Minimum Service Package, shared assessment instruments, and agreed referral pathways — with supervision and training routed to whoever can provide it.

📈

Information management

Common activity codes, one site roster, a form a field worker can fill on a phone with no signal, and a dashboard that shows the coverage gap rather than only the total reached.

Method

How the numbers are built, and what they refuse to guess

The counting rules are published rather than assumed: reach is unique individuals, a contact is a session, and the two are never added. Activity codes follow the IASC 4Ws where the official list is available and are left empty where it is not, rather than invented. A site that appears in a report but not on the roster is shown separately and never counted as a gap.
