§1 What is being measured, and against what

A total can look healthy while a whole holding centre has nobody. So the measure is not the total reached — it is coverage against the official roster, and the gap is the point.

Every site in the response, one square each

A square is one site code in the response's site list. A square is filled when at least one organisation has reported an MHPSS activity there. None is filled, because no organisation has reported yet — so this figure is, for now, the gap itself.

Counting rules — reach is unique individuals; a service contact is a session. The two are never added together. A site that appears in a report but is not on the roster is shown separately and is never counted as a gap. Activity codes follow the IASC 4Ws where the official list is available, and are left empty where it is not, rather than invented.

§2 Where this sits in the MHPSS response

MHPSS is not one service. The IASC Guidelines describe a layered system of complementary supports, and a response is read on whether all four layers are present — not on the total number of people seen. The activity codes on the field form are grouped to these layers, so coverage can be read layer by layer rather than as a single number.

The IASC intervention pyramid

All layers are important and should ideally be implemented concurrently. The pyramid is wide at the bottom because that is where most people are reached, not because the bottom layer matters more.

  1. 4
    Specialised services

    The small percentage whose suffering is intolerable and who may have significant difficulty in basic daily functioning. A small share of the population — but in a large emergency, still thousands of people.

  2. 3
    Focused, non-specialised supports

    More focused individual, family or group interventions by trained and supervised workers: psychological first aid, and basic mental health care by primary health care workers.

  3. 2
    Community and family supports

    Family tracing and reunification, assisted mourning and communal healing ceremonies, mass communication on constructive coping, supportive parenting, formal and non-formal education, livelihoods, and the activation of social networks.

  4. 1
    Basic services and security

    Security, adequate governance and services that address basic physical needs — food, shelter, water, basic health care, control of communicable diseases — through which the well-being of all people is protected.

What this figure says

Ten of the fifteen activity codes on the field form sit in layers 2, 3 and 4. Nothing on the form records layer 1, because basic services and security are delivered by the other clusters. So this system can show whether MHPSS reached a holding centre — it cannot show whether that centre has water. The two have to be read side by side, and a coverage figure from here should never be presented as a picture of the whole response.

Source — IASC, Guidelines on Mental Health and Psychosocial Support in Emergency Settings, 2007: the intervention pyramid figure and the description of each layer. The four layer names are as printed there. Later renderings of the figure label the layers differently; this page keeps the 2007 wording.

Five codes sit across the layers rather than in one. Referral is a pathway between layers, not a layer; a helpline contact is a channel, and the support given over it belongs to whichever layer it came from; rapid assessment, coordination meetings and training are system functions. The published mapping of the MHPSS Minimum Service Package onto the pyramid marks coordination and assessment as not applicable to a single layer for the same reason. The grouping is this response's own. The official IASC 4Ws activity code list has not been obtained, so no code on the form carries an official IASC number — the field is present and left empty rather than filled with a guess.

MHPSS describes any type of local or outside support that aims to protect or promote psychosocial well-being, or prevent or treat mental disorders. IASC Guidelines on Mental Health and Psychosocial Support in Emergency Settings, 2007

§3 Who takes part

The sub-cluster is open to government bodies, UN agencies, international and national NGOs, professional associations, academic departments, and any organisation or individual active in mental health and psychosocial support in this response.

Convened under the Health Cluster

  • Ministry of Health and Food Safety (MoHP)
  • Epidemiology and Disease Control Division — NCD & Mental Health Section
  • District health offices, Rasuwa and Nuwakot
  • WHO Nepal

Reporting partners

  • Organisations reporting MHPSS activity in the response
  • Professional associations and academic departments

Cross-sector, and why it matters here

MHPSS in Nepal has long been coordinated through two tracks — a mental health sub-cluster under the Health Cluster, and a psychosocial working group under Protection. Organisations whose primary mandate is not MHPSS but whose work carries mental health or psychosocial components are asked to attend and contribute:

  • Protection and child protection
  • Gender-based violence services
  • Education, nutrition, camp management

One shared table does not ask anyone to leave their own.

Membership is listed by category, not by name, until the sub-cluster confirms its own participant list. Naming an organisation before it has agreed to be named would be a claim, not a record.

§6 Three layers, one direction

Data moves one way. It is entered once in the field, interpreted at coordination level, and published only as aggregates. Nothing on the public layer can reach back down into a record about a person.

What the model refuses to guess

  • Activity codes are left empty where the official IASC list has not been obtained
  • No automatic Bikram Sambat conversion, because no verified conversion table is held
  • A reported site that is not on the roster is shown apart, never counted as a gap
  • The register refuses names, phone numbers, dates of birth, national IDs and precise locations
