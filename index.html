§1 What is being measured, and against what

A total can look healthy while a whole holding centre has nobody. So the measure is not the total reached — it is coverage against the official roster, and the gap is the point.

The four figures the dashboard is built to produce, and the state of each.

Counting rules — reach is unique individuals; a service contact is a session. The two are never added together. A site that appears in a report but is not on the roster is shown separately and is never counted as a gap. Activity codes follow the IASC 4Ws where the official list is available, and are left empty where it is not, rather than invented.

§2 Who takes part

The sub-cluster is open to government bodies, UN agencies, international and national NGOs, professional associations, academic departments, and any organisation or individual active in mental health and psychosocial support in this response.

Convened under the Health Cluster

  • Ministry of Health and Food Safety (MoHP)
  • Epidemiology and Disease Control Division — NCD & Mental Health Section
  • District health offices, Rasuwa and Nuwakot
  • WHO Nepal

Reporting partners

  • Organisations reporting MHPSS activity in the response
  • Professional associations and academic departments

Cross-sector, and why it matters here

MHPSS in Nepal has long been coordinated through two tracks — a mental health sub-cluster under the Health Cluster, and a psychosocial working group under Protection. Organisations whose primary mandate is not MHPSS but whose work carries mental health or psychosocial components are asked to attend and contribute:

  • Protection and child protection
  • Gender-based violence services
  • Education, nutrition, camp management

One shared table does not ask anyone to leave their own.

Membership is listed by category, not by name, until the sub-cluster confirms its own participant list. Naming an organisation before it has agreed to be named would be a claim, not a record.

§5 Three layers, one direction

Data moves one way. It is entered once in the field, interpreted at coordination level, and published only as aggregates. Nothing on the public layer can reach back down into a record about a person.

What the model refuses to guess

  • Activity codes are left empty where the official IASC list has not been obtained
  • No automatic Bikram Sambat conversion, because no verified conversion table is held
  • A reported site that is not on the roster is shown apart, never counted as a gap
  • The register refuses names, phone numbers, dates of birth, national IDs and precise locations
