Who can see what

Two questions come up whenever a reporting system is put in front of clinical teams: will my staff be locked out of reporting, and could a client's details end up somewhere they should not be. This page answers both, and says plainly what the system does not protect against.

THE SHORT ANSWERReporting is open. Reading is not.

These are two separate things, and they are deliberately treated differently.

Anyone providing a service

Filling in a form

Needs nothing at all

A counsellor opens the link, fills in the form, and saves. No account. No email address. No password.

A psychosocial worker in a holding centre, on a borrowed phone, with no signal, can complete a report and it will be kept on the handset until a connection returns. Nobody has to set them up first, and nothing about access control applies to them.

Anyone looking at what was reported

Reading the records

Must be named, one person at a time

Reading service records is a coordination function, so the system asks two things: who are you, and were you given access.

Signing in only answers the first. An account with no access granted to it sees nothing at all — not a partial view, not a summary. Access is granted by name, by the coordinator, and can be withdrawn the same way.

HOW IT WORKSTwo checkpoints, not one

A record passes a check on the way in, and a person passes a different check on the way to reading it. The first applies to everybody, including senior staff.

CHECKPOINT 1What the system will not accept

This is the part worth dwelling on with partners. These details are not discouraged by guidance or training — the system refuses to store them. If a form were changed tomorrow to ask for a client's name, the record would still be turned away.

Refused whatever the form asks for, and whoever submits it Checked at the point of storage, so a change to a form cannot quietly widen what is collected. This applies to the people reporting as well as the people being reported on.
  • A person's namein any field, in any spelling
  • A phone numberclient's or family member's
  • A date of birthage band only, never the date
  • A citizenship or national ID number
  • A home address or household identifier
  • A photograph or scanned document
  • A name typed into the coded-reference fieldthe code has a fixed shape, so a name will not fit it
  • A location precise enough to identify a dwellingrecorded to roughly a 100 m area, and only inside Nepal
  • A date set by the handset rather than the systemso a report cannot be back-dated
  • Free text longer than a short notelong narrative is where identities leak in
  • The reporting focal point's own name, phone or emaila named professional; stays on the handset and in the private file sent to coordination, never in the register

Nobody can raise their own level, and no web page can change anyone's level — including an administrator's own. Correcting a figure is not the same as removing an entry: a correction is submitted as a new record, so the reporting history stays intact and a changed number can always be traced. Removal exists only for genuine mistakes — a duplicate, or a test entry — and is why it sits with the administrator alone.

ASSURANCEHow this was checked

Not reviewed on paper — attempted in practice. Each check is a deliberate attempt to get something through that should not get through.

The fault is worth naming, because it shows the checks are not cosmetic: an early version locked out the coordinator himself. It was found by testing, not in use. The checks are re-run automatically with every backup — if the protections were ever loosened, the backup reports it rather than quietly succeeding.

LIMITSWhat this does not protect against

Stated here rather than left to be discovered, because a system described as safer than it is will be trusted with more than it should hold.

Three things to be clear about

Anyone with the link can submit a report.
Reporting is deliberately open, so submissions are not verified as coming from a real service. What that risks is inaccurate counts, not disclosure — nothing identifying can be stored whoever submits it. If false entries ever appear, the answer is a sign-in for each reporting organisation, not a change to these checks.
A coded reference is a pseudonym, not anonymity.
It is designed so the same person can be recognised across visits without being named. Someone who already holds the key linking codes to people could still connect them — which is why that key stays with the service that created it and never enters this system.
A reporting focal point is still contactable — just not through this system.
A 4Ws report normally names someone so a figure can be queried later. That name and number stay on the reporting worker's phone and in the file they send to coordination; the register itself does not keep them. So querying a figure goes through the organisation or the file, not through a lookup here. This was tightened on 15 September after testing showed the register had been accepting them.
This protects records in the system, not conversations around it.
A name spoken in a coordination meeting, written in a WhatsApp message, or kept in a service's own case notes is outside anything described here. The system can hold the line on what it stores; it cannot hold the line on practice.

IN PRACTICEWhat this means for a partner organisation

For your field staff

Nothing to set up

Send them the form link. That is the whole onboarding. They need no account and you do not need to ask us for anything on their behalf.

The form works with no signal and keeps the report on the handset until there is one.

For your focal point

One email address

Give the coordinator the email address of the person who needs to see the records. They will be sent a sign-in link — no password is chosen, typed or shared, by them or by anyone holding it on their behalf.

Access can be withdrawn immediately if they change post.
